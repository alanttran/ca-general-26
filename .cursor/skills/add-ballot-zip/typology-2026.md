# Pew 2026 political typology: reference for picks

Source: Pew Research Center, “Beyond Red vs. Blue: The Political Typology” (June 10, 2026; survey of 10,357 adults, Nov 17–30, 2025, plus linked panel waves through April 2026) and “How political typology groups feel about the midterm elections and Trump” (July 23, 2026; survey July 6–12, 2026).
Overview: https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/06/10/beyond-red-vs-blue-the-political-typology/

These nine groups **replace** the 2021 groups (Progressive Left … Faith and Flag Conservatives). They are new clusters, not renamed old ones: do not carry over 2021 reasoning. Derive each pick from the 2026 profile below.

Numbers are approximate where read from Pew charts (±2 points).

## Profiles and how each group weighs a ballot choice

### LP: Leftward Progressives (7%)
Youngest, most secular, highest LGBTQ share (36%), majority White, college-educated, very online. Liberal on nearly everything and the most intense: economic system unfair (92%), billionaires bad for the country (82%), 63% say being extremely rich is morally wrong, 66% like democratic socialists. Most skeptical of police (28% confidence) and of U.S. military power; least concerned about violent crime (17%). Most pro-trans-rights. Want more parties (70%); only 61% favorable to the Democratic Party; want Democrats to “push back” on Trump, not find common ground.
**Picks:** the most progressive viable option; tax the rich and corporations; expansive safety net; tenant protections and public/social housing; decarceration, police accountability; strongest climate action; labor. Skeptical of moderate/business-backed Democrats, corporate- or billionaire-funded measures, and institutional insiders when a credible left alternative exists. Must pick the Democrat over a Republican (build rule).

### LL: Loyal Liberals (11%)
Highly educated (61% college+), economically secure, 73% White, most engaged group alongside NA (86% say Congress control really matters). Strongly attached to the Democratic Party (81% say it represents them well; 45% strong Democrats). Progressive on abortion, same-sex marriage, race, immigration (majority want more legal immigration), but less comfortable than LP with trans athletes (28%). Believe the current system can deliver prosperity (far more than LP). Highest trust in institutions, national news (82%), scientists (67% great deal), experts (92%). Pro-alliance, pro-NATO. 92% raise corporate taxes; 80% expand government services.
**Picks:** mainstream Democratic and institutional choices: party/labor/editorial endorsements, experience, expert-backed policy (LAO, scientists, public-health), competent governance, incremental reform through institutions. Accept bonds and taxes for services. Weigh ethics findings seriously (institutional trust cuts both ways). Must pick the Democrat over a Republican (build rule).

### LO: Left-Out Left (12%)
Young (74% under 50), working class (65%; 70% no BA), racially diverse (46% White, 25% Hispanic, 14% Black). Financially stressed. Feel politically ignored: only 53% say a candidate has shared their views lately; the only group where fewer than half say voting gives people like them a say; 75% say few or no officials ran to serve the public (highest), many say most ran to make money. 57% say the country can’t solve its problems. Economically center-left/populist: govt should guarantee healthcare, retirement income, K-12; distrust large corporations; bothered that the wealthy don’t pay their share (77%); 46% say billionaires are bad. Socially moderate-left: accept same-sex marriage (but weakly), mixed on gender identity, most have reservations about trans athletes. Moderate on immigration (path to legal status, 38% say border security extremely/very important, oppose detention/deportation drives). Only 22% like democratic socialists. Lukewarm toward Democrats, cold toward Republicans (74% cold vs 35%). Middling police trust (49%).
**Picks:** economic populism and cost-of-living relief for working people; anti-corruption, anti-insider, anti-self-dealing; outsiders over machine candidates when the outsider is credible; deeply skeptical of politicians’ motives, so red flags about money and ethics weigh heavily. Not ideological purity: practical help beats left labels. Usually Democratic in D-vs-R races but not automatic; justify. Low trust that measures will be spent as promised: wants accountability and specifics.

### OO: Order and Opportunity Left (18%, largest group)
Racially diverse (41% White, 26% Hispanic, 21% Black, 8% Asian), fairly religious (71% say religion matters), economically pinched, many lack emergency savings. Less engaged (46% voted in 2024; 18% very interested). 65% Democratic-leaning, 25% Republican-leaning; 59% moderate. Economically liberal (bigger government, healthcare, corporations make too much profit, raise corporate taxes 73%) but with more economic individualism and more confidence in business (40%) than groups to their left. Want order and safety: violent crime a very big problem (52%), more trust in police (69%) and military, 74% call themselves “respectful of authority,” 32% “traditional.” Moderate on immigration: 59% favor military presence at the border, 30% see illegal immigration as a very big problem, but oppose mass deportation, detention and asylum suspension. Socially moderate: liberal-ish on abortion (69% legal), mixed on same-sex marriage (51% neutral), 71% say gender is set at birth, yet 62% see heavy discrimination against trans people. 84% back photo voter ID but also back automatic registration (65%) and no-excuse mail voting (70%).
**Picks:** practical, pro-worker economics plus public safety and order: tough-on-violent-crime and pro-police-funding positions, opportunity (jobs, homeownership, small business), affordability. Prefer steady, competent, mainstream candidates; uncomfortable with both far-left policing views and MAGA hard-line immigration. Usually Democratic in D-vs-R races, but a moderate Republican focused on crime/costs can be a defensible fit (justify). Voter-ID measure: likely Yes.

### TM: Tuned-Out Middle (9%)
Least engaged (32% voted in 2024; 46% rarely follow politics; only 30% see big party differences). Most financially strained (56% lower income; half struggle with bills), least college (half HS or less), most racially diverse (one-third White, ~quarter Hispanic, ~quarter Black), young. Distrustful (68% say most people can’t be trusted); 54% feel unfairly judged for how they express themselves. Partisanship split (46 D / 43 R), 52% moderate, 31% conservative, 13% liberal. Modestly favor bigger government and the safety net (73% govt responsible for adequate living standard; support Social Security, government healthcare). More conservative on same-sex marriage (46% bad for society) and gender identity. Middle on climate, immigration (want both border security and a path to legal status), abortion (48% legal).
**Picks:** pocketbook first: direct help with costs, jobs, wages, benefits; wary of new taxes that hit them; tune out ideological fights; social conservatism shows on LGBTQ items. Least party-driven: choose by concrete material benefit and plain trustworthiness. `—` is acceptable when nothing on the ballot plausibly touches their priorities, but prefer a pick with ◐/○ when one side clearly offers more cost relief.

### PP: Pragmatic and Polite Right (11%)
Oldest group (68% 50+, 42% 65+), fairly religious (53% say religion very important), fairly diverse for its age (65% White, 13% Hispanic, 9% Black, 9% Asian). 56% Republican-leaning, 37% Democratic-leaning; half moderate. Voted Trump 54–40 in 2024 but only 34–36% approve now; 49% dislike his conduct; 54% unfavorable to the GOP and 67% unfavorable to Democrats. Defining trait is civility and caution: 86% say officials should avoid heated language, 78% prefer incremental fixes over risky quick ones, 70% dislike politicians humiliating opponents a lot, 72% say offensive speech is a major problem. Economically conservative but not absolutist: capitalism important, system fair-ish, small-government streak, yet 61% raise corporate taxes and they see inequality as a problem. Moderate-to-liberal on race (71% diversity strengthens), oppose mass deportation (two-thirds back a path to legal status) though border security matters (71% favor military presence). Pro-alliance, pro-NATO, pro-Ukraine aid (59%). 57% say gun violence is a very big problem; 84% confidence in police; 83% gender set at birth.
**Picks:** temperament, competence, experience, fiscal prudence and the rule of law over party. Reward measured, bipartisan, incremental candidates; penalize extremism, insult politics, election denial, conspiracy-mongering and misconduct. Mild preference for lower taxes and limited spending, but will back targeted, well-run spending and cautious reforms. In D-vs-R races, often the Republican if mainstream; a moderate Democrat if the Republican is MAGA-aligned, combative or unqualified. Most likely group to split tickets.

### UR: Unconventional Right (12%)
Younger and less religious than other right groups; least engaged right-leaning group (55% voted 2024; 43% say Congress control really matters). 76% Republican-leaning but only 26% strong Republicans; 46% conservative, 46% moderate. Trump approval fell from 78% to ~51%, the steepest drop. Conservative on immigration (56% oppose legal status; 50% back national deportation effort; 85% favor military at border) and on race/diversity (45% diversity strengthens; skeptical of DEI), gender identity (87% sex at birth). But mixed or moderate elsewhere: 55% abortion legal; less opposed to same-sex marriage; uncomfortable with open carry (24%) though gun violence not seen as a big problem (28%); split on government healthcare (48/51); favorable to NATO (52%). Small-government in general. Less concerned with inoffensive language (47% say it doesn’t matter).
**Picks:** border/immigration enforcement, anti-DEI, lower taxes and smaller government, crime; libertarian-ish on personal choices (abortion, marijuana), not religious-right. Lukewarm on Trump personally and on party loyalty; respond to anti-establishment, plain-spoken candidates. Usually the Republican in D-vs-R, but with less conviction (◐ common), and a moderate Democrat strong on crime/costs can win a ○/◐ when the Republican is weak.

### FC: Faith First Conservatives (12%)
Most religious and most Christian group (72% say religion very important; regularly attend services); 53% say religion shapes how they vote. One of the most rural (41%). 95% Republican-leaning; 81% then 77% Trump approval; 90% support MAGA. Strongly anti-abortion (83% illegal in most cases; ~65% say medication abortion should be illegal), 76% say same-sex marriage bad for society, 82% homosexuality morally wrong, 97% sex at birth; 82% want a Christian-based national culture. Pro-family (64% say fewer births bad; support government encouraging children). Small government: ~75% want services reduced, 75% say healthcare not government’s job. Restrictive on immigration (69% oppose legal status; 72% illegal immigration very big problem) though less hard-line than NA. Pro-gun (55% comfortable with open carry, 37% want looser gun laws). Less combative than NA (27% like humiliating opponents).
**Picks:** social conservatism first (abortion, parental rights, religious liberty, LGBTQ curriculum/trans issues), then limited government, low taxes, law and order, border security. Usually the Republican; character and personal-morality red flags matter to them. On measures: oppose tax increases and social-liberal measures; may back law-and-order measures and some family/veterans measures.

### NA: No Apologies Right (9%)
Most Republican (99%), most pro-Trump (89–90% approve; 63% say best president of the last 40 years), most engaged right-leaning group (83% voted 2024; 84% say Congress control really matters). 74% men (most gender-tilted group). Hard-line on almost everything: 81% back a national deportation effort, 75% back suspending asylum; strongest opponents of DEI; most likely to say White people face discrimination; most say slavery’s legacy has no effect. Small government and deregulation: ~80% say regulation does more harm than good (only group with a clear majority); three-quarters want less aid to the needy; 86% say healthcare not government’s job; only 20% raise corporate taxes. Pro-gun (70% open carry; 55% looser gun laws). Climate skeptics (~two-thirds: not a problem) and most skeptical of experts and scientists (33% experts better). Confrontational style: 53% like seeing opponents humiliated; 70% say avoiding offensive language is unimportant; least interested in Trump working with Democrats. Individualists: most likely to say people control their own financial success.
**Picks:** MAGA-aligned, combative conservatives; deregulation, tax cuts, border enforcement, anti-DEI, gun rights, anti-climate-mandate, tough on crime. Distrust experts, LAO-style institutional authority, and mainstream media. Prefer fighters over moderates, even within the GOP. Opposes nearly all new taxes and most bonds.

## Rules of thumb for deriving picks

1. **Values first, party as tiebreak.** Decide from each group’s 2026 issue positions and style. Party lean breaks only near-ties. Mixed groups (LO, OO, TM, PP, UR) should cross party lines when a candidate or measure fits their values better; say why.
2. **Build rule:** in a race with a Democrat (or Green/P&F) against a Republican, LP and LL must pick the Democrat. No other column is forced.
3. **Same-party races:** pick on ideology, establishment vs outsider, style and record; say in the rationale which finalist is further left/right or more insider/outsider and why that fits the group.
4. **Nonpartisan local races:** use endorsements (party, labor, police, business, DSA, etc.), positions and record to place candidates; map to group values (e.g. OO and PP lean toward police-endorsed and steady candidates; LP toward tenant/labor/left; LO toward anti-insider populists; NA toward combative conservatives).
5. **Style and red flags are values here:** PP penalizes incivility, extremism, election denial and misconduct heavily; LO and TM distrust insiders and self-dealing; NA tolerates combativeness and dismisses “establishment” ethics attacks less than you might expect but still not crimes. A severe flag can never get ● (build rule).
6. **Rationales** are one sentence, third person about the group (“Order and Opportunity Left voters want …”), specific to this race, citing the value from this doc that drives the pick. Use the full 2026 group name, never a 2021 name.
7. **Confidence:** ● clear fit; ◐ defensible trade-off; ○ weak lean; `—` skip (only when nothing on the ballot reasonably maps to the group’s priorities).
